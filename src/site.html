About

I build things that have to survive contact with the physical world

Compilers, telescopes, and the models that decide what they’re looking at

Bench is where the work gets written down. The name is the literal one — a bench is where a thing is built, measured, and found wanting before anyone else sees it, and that is the part worth publishing.

I work across three domains that don’t usually share a portfolio. Software: compilers, build systems, developer tools. Hardware: motion control, sensing, things with a bill of materials. Machine learning: mostly applied, mostly on-device, mostly outdoors.

The through-line is that each one produces a claim about the physical world, and a claim needs evidence. So every project here carries its own headline number in its own units — a build speedup, a tracking error in arcseconds, an F1 score — and links to the writing that shows the work.

A specification that does not say what it assumes is not a specification. It is a description of one run.

How this is set

Everything is one serif family: a transitional for display and an old-style for reading, with italic doing the work that colour usually does. There is no second typeface and no accent hue — hierarchy comes from scale, italic, and the weight of a rule.

The illustrations are hand-drawn SVG line plates rather than photographs. For hardware that is the native representation anyway: a dimensioned orthographic drawing carries more than a snapshot of a breadboard would.

  • DisplayPlayfair / Hoefler Text
  • ReadingIowan Old Style
  • Measure38rem / ~66 characters
  • ColourNone
  • PlatesInline SVG, one stroke
